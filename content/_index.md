I am an Economics Ph.D. candidate at the University of Delaware. My research uses causal inference methods to study labor economics and household finance, focusing on how work-from-home arrangements and financial markets shape households' labor supply and financial well-being.

My job market paper examines how access to work from home affects mothers' labor supply during summer school closures. My third-year paper, on online gambling legalization and household credit card debt, is published in the Journal of Gambling Studies. Before my doctoral studies, I earned my B.A. and M.A. in Economics at Istanbul University and worked in the Turkish fintech sector. I hold the YLSY Fellowship, a fully funded scholarship from the Turkish Ministry of National Education.
